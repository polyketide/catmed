# Steroid Duration in Feline Lymphoma Protocols — designed in advance, never decided by response, and what stopping abruptly actually risks

> Generated 2026-09-06 · PubMed search + verbatim verification · Not diagnostic, not a prescription
> **Purpose**: prompted by a real course in which prednisolone was stopped mid-protocol after a deep response, chemotherapy continuing — and the owner asked whether that loses something. The literature's answer is stranger than either yes or no: **steroid duration in published feline protocols is a design constant, not a response-triggered decision, and the comparison "stop versus continue after remission" has never been studied in any species.**

## 1. What published protocols actually do with prednisolone

Two designs coexist in the feline literature, and neither is argued for — each is simply a protocol constant:

| Design | Protocol | Steroid schedule | Outcome context |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Full-length daily** | Modified UW-25, 119 cats (Collette 2016, PMID 26109275) | ⚠️ Per the paper's protocol table: prednisolone daily *"continued through the protocol to be tapered upon completion"*, with earlier tapering allowed *"at the discretion of the clinician"* 【from the full text (Table A1 footnote), not the abstract — verify before relying on it】 | Median PFI 56 / MST 97 days overall; CR cats far longer (see §3 of [`response-assessment-and-drug-resistance.md`](response-assessment-and-drug-resistance.md)) |
| **Early taper-off, chemo continues** | CMOP, 31 cats (Webster 2024, PMID 38647264) | ⚠️ Per the protocol table: started at 2 mg/kg, halved by week 3, thereafter optional at clinician's discretion 【full text, not abstract — verify】 | CR 45%; CR cats' MST 1176 days |
| **Early taper-off, chemo continues** | COVP, 41 cats (Pui Yung 2025, PMID 40119555) | ⚠️ Per the methods: started 1.5–2 mg/kg daily, tapered off over 3–4 weeks while injectable agents continue to week 26 【full text, not abstract — verify】 | CR 59%; DFI 812 days; CR cats 838 vs 143 days (P = 0.0018) |

⭐ **Consequence**: a clinician who stops prednisolone after induction while chemotherapy continues is doing something that two modern published protocols do **by design**, with unremarkable-to-good outcomes. It is inside the envelope of published practice. What it is *not* is a validated strategy — no protocol stops the steroid *because* remission was achieved, and no study has compared stopping with continuing.

## 2. The comparison that does not exist — and the indirect evidence on each side

**"After remission: continue the steroid versus stop it, chemotherapy identical" has never been studied in cats, dogs, or (within a chemotherapy protocol) humans.** Recorded as a negative result of this search.

**Toward "continuing has a cost" (mechanism level, in vitro):** in feline lymphoma cell lines, prednisolone pre-treatment *reduced* subsequent killing by doxorubicin or vincristine and upregulated multidrug-resistance-associated mRNA (Hlavaty 2021, PMID 34157499). ⚠️ Cell lines, pre-treatment design — a mechanism direction, not a clinical measurement.

**The adjacent (and often conflated) canine question — steroids *before* chemotherapy:** the clinical evidence is contradictory. Pre-treated dogs did worse in two datasets (Maga 2025, PMID 40624957 — though dose and duration had no impact, which the authors note; Lautscham 2017, PMID 28100766, multivariate), and pre-treatment made no difference in a third (Wiedemann 2005, PMID 15835237). ⚠️ **All three are about steroid exposure *before* induction — none addresses stopping during remission.** The two questions travel together in conversation and must not in reasoning.

## 3. Stopping abruptly: what feline physiology actually shows

- **Suppression is fast.** In cats given prednisolone ≥2 mg/kg/day, ACTH-stimulated cortisol was significantly suppressed by **day 8**, deeper by day 15 — after only 16 days of dosing (Middleton 1987, PMID 3032391).
- **Recovery is usually quick and silent.** Adrenal reserve was judged recovered ~2 weeks after stopping in 6 of 7 prednisolone cats (Middleton 1987). The authors nonetheless advise avoiding abrupt withdrawal in this species, *"even after treatment for as little as one week"*.
- **Clinical crisis is rare in cats.** The species is described as *"remarkably resistant"* to prolonged glucocorticoid administration, with only two published clinical cases of iatrogenic Cushing's at the time of that report (Ferasin 2001, PMID 11876622) — and published feline cases of clinically significant iatrogenic hypoadrenocorticism cluster around **long-acting depot injections**, not oral prednisolone. **A published feline case of clinical adrenal crisis after abrupt oral prednisolone withdrawal was not found** (negative search result).
- **The human calibration** (⚠️ human data): after glucocorticoid discontinuation, biochemical adrenal insufficiency is frequent and cannot be excluded for any dose, duration or route with certainty; risk scales with dose and duration (Broersen 2015, PMID 25844620).

⭐ **The practical asymmetry**: a cat whose axis is suppressed but recovering will usually be fine on a quiet day at home — the exposure is a **stress event** (a clinic visit, an intercurrent illness) in the first weeks after stopping, which is precisely when the animal cannot mount a normal cortisol response. New lethargy, vomiting, anorexia or collapse in the weeks after an abrupt stop belongs on the differential as *possible adrenal insufficiency*, not only as "chemotherapy side effect" — the presentations are look-alikes, which is what makes the miss easy.

⚠️ **What this does *not* mean.** It does not mean abrupt stopping was wrong in any particular course (protocols above stop early by design; cats mostly recover uneventfully), and it does not mean owners should re-dose steroids around stressful events on their own — whether a stress-day dose is warranted is a physiology-grounded question **for the veterinarian**.

## 4. One further consequence: the blood count changes meaning

Glucocorticoids raise the neutrophil count and depress eosinophils (the classic stress/steroid leukogram). While a cat is on daily prednisolone, a normal-looking neutrophil count can conceal marrow suppression; after withdrawal and washout, the same panel becomes a cleaner read of the marrow itself. ⚠️ Standard clinical pathology, stated here as reasoning, not sourced to a specific feline study — flagged so it is not mistaken for a verified quantitative claim.

**PMID 26109275** · Collette SA 2016
> The Kaplan-Meier median progression-free interval (PFI) and survival time (MST) were 56 and 97 (range 2-2019) days, respectively.
> Cats assessed as having a complete response (CR) to therapy had significantly longer PFI and MST than those with partial or no response
> ⚠️ The prednisolone schedule ("continued through the protocol to be tapered upon completion"; earlier tapering "at the discretion of the clinician") is in the full-text protocol table (Table A1), not the abstract.

**PMID 38647264** · Webster J 2024
> The overall response rate was 74% (n = 23), with 45% (n = 14) achieving complete remission (CR), 29% (n = 9) achieving partial remission (PR) and 26% (n = 8) achieving stable disease (SD).
> Cats that achieved CR had a significantly longer median PFI and MST (P &lt;0.001) at 178 days and 1176 days, respectively.
> ⚠️ The prednisolone schedule (2 mg/kg week 1, tapered to 1 mg/kg by week 3, thereafter discretionary) is in the full-text protocol table, not the abstract.

**PMID 40119555** · Pui Yung Anna L 2025
> Progression-free survival was 264 days (range, 6-1486 days), the disease-free interval was 812 days (range, 39-1486 days) and the median survival time for all cats was 412 days (range, 7-1772 days).
> Complete response was achieved in 59% of the cases, and partial response was observed in 17%.
> Cats that achieved CR lived significantly longer, 838 days (range, 81-1772 days) versus 143 days (range, 10-798 days; p = 0.0018).
> ⚠️ The prednisolone schedule (1.5–2 mg/kg daily, tapered off over 3–4 weeks while injectable chemotherapy continues) is in the full-text methods, not the abstract.

**PMID 34157499** · Hlavaty J 2021
> Using feline lymphoma cell lines FT-1 and MS4 we have shown, that prednisolone pre-treatment alters the susceptibility of these cells towards doxorubicin or vincristine treatment in vitro.
> The observed effect was negative as for the killing potential and it was cell line and drug (doxorubicin or vincristine) dependent.
> Furthermore, increase in mRNA expression of selected proteins with multidrug resistance potential (MDR1, BCRP, LRP, MT) was observed after prednisolone pre-treatment.

**PMID 40624957** · Maga I 2025
> Steroid dose, duration, and cumulative dose had no significant impact on TTP or LSS.
> These findings suggest that the detrimental effects of upfront steroids are independent of dose or duration.

**PMID 28100766** · Lautscham EM 2017
> There was no significant difference between the two protocols (P=0.291 for ST, P=0.071 for DFR).
> This study supports the use of a discontinuous protocol for canine multicentric lymphoma.

**PMID 15835237** · Wiedemann AL 2005
> sALP was not statistically significant with respect to response rate or duration of remission, nor was preinduction glucocorticoid administration.

**PMID 3032391** · Middleton DJ 1987
> Cats given prednisolone or megestrol acetate had significant suppression of stimulated cortisol levels on day 8.
> Recovery of adrenal reserve was considered present on day 30 in six of seven cats given prednisolone, but in only three of seven receiving megestrol acetate.
> It is advised that abrupt withdrawal of prednisolone or megestrol acetate therapy be avoided in this species to reduce the chance of precipitating clinical signs of hypoadrenocorticism, even after treatment for as little as one week.

**PMID 11876622** · Ferasin L 2001
> Whilst iatrogenic hyperadrenocorticism is relatively frequent in dogs, the diagnosis of iatrogenic hyperadrenocorticism in cats is very uncommon because this species has been found to be remarkably resistant to prolonged administration of glucocorticoids.
> To the author's knowledge, there are only two published clinical cases of feline iatrogenic Cushing's syndrome.

**PMID 25844620** · Broersen LHA 2015
> Adrenal insufficiency after discontinuation of glucocorticoid occurs frequently
> there is no administration form, dosing, treatment duration, or underlying disease for which adrenal insufficiency can be excluded with certainty, although higher dose and longer use give the highest risk
