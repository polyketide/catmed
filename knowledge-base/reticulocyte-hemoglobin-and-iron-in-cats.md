# Reticulocyte Hemoglobin and Iron in Cats — a cutoff that belongs to a different analyzer, and supplementation that rests on tradition

> Generated 2026-09-06 · PubMed search + verbatim verification · Not diagnostic, not a prescription
> **Purpose**: prompted by a real course in which a cat's in-clinic RETIC-HGB readings were repeatedly interpreted against "the feline iron-deficiency cutoff of 15.9 pg" — until a full-text check showed that number was derived on a **different analyzer**, in a **different patient population**, and performs as an **exclusion tool rather than a diagnostic one**. The error is easy to make, spreads quietly through notes, and this file exists so it does not have to be rediscovered.
> **Division of labour**: chemotherapy-associated cytopenias live in [`antineoplastic-drug-toxicity.md`](antineoplastic-drug-toxicity.md); CKD-associated anemia and ESAs in [`chronic-kidney-disease.md`](chronic-kidney-disease.md). This file is about **reading reticulocyte iron indices correctly and what iron supplementation in cats actually rests on**.

## 1. The cross-analyzer trap — the reason this file exists

Two numbers circulate for "iron-limited erythropoiesis" in cats, and they come from **two different machines**:

| Figure | Analyzer | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **CHr cutoff 15.9 pg** (sensitivity 67%, specificity 82%) | Siemens **ADVIA 2120i** | Betting 2022 (PMID 35090061), 64 cats with CKD ± subcutaneous ureteral bypass |
| **RETIC-HGB reference interval 12.5–18.0 pg** | IDEXX **ProCyte Dx** | Keiner 2020 (PMID 33617045), RIs from 59 healthy cats |

The two parameters are **moderately correlated at best** (rs = 0.59) with a **bias of −1.2 pg**, and the Keiner authors attribute the divergence to species-specific methodology (Keiner 2020). ⭐ **Consequence: a ProCyte RETIC-HGB of 13.8–14.4 pg sits *inside* the ProCyte reference interval (low end). Reading it as "below the feline iron-deficiency cutoff of 15.9" silently imports an ADVIA number into a ProCyte report.** No study validates that transfer.

Three further restraints on the 15.9 figure itself, from its own source:

- The study population was CKD/SUB cats with chronic hematuria; cats with acute illness or active inflammatory disease were excluded — a chemotherapy patient is the kind of cat that population does **not** represent.
- Prevalence in the study was 14% (9/64), and ⚠️ the full text reports a positive predictive value of 38% against a negative predictive value of 94% — i.e. it is an **exclusion** tool. 【figure from the full text, not the abstract — verify against the original before relying on it】
- Discrimination was **moderate** by the authors' own description (AUC 0.75, CI 0.48–0.89).

⚠️ **What this does *not* mean.** A low-end RETIC-HGB is not meaningless — in the ProCyte validation itself, cats with iron-limited erythropoiesis had significantly lower RETIC-HGB than those without, and using the RI lower limit was specific but insensitive (Keiner 2020). Low-end values keep iron limitation **on the table**; they do not put a cat **below a validated cutoff**.

## 2. What iron supplementation in cats actually rests on

**No controlled efficacy trial of iron supplementation exists in cats — for any indication, any formulation.** The doses in circulation are review-level tradition, not trial results:

- Oral ferrous sulfate 50–100 mg/cat/day (≈10–20 mg elemental iron), with poor gastrointestinal absorption and a bitter taste cats may refuse (Chalhoub 2011, PMID 21872790).
- Iron dextran 50 mg/cat IM every 3–4 weeks, with a small test dose first because hypersensitivity reactions can occur — and **never intravenously** (anaphylaxis risk) (Chalhoub 2011; Naigamwalla 2012, PMID 22942439).
- ⚠️ **All of the dose figures above come from the body text of narrative reviews, not from their PubMed abstracts, and not from trials** — verify against the full reviews before relying on them, and treat them as a starting point for a conversation with the veterinarian, not a regimen.
- IV iron sucrose has a 5-healthy-cat safety study only: no acute reactions, but a transient rise in serum amyloid A, and the authors state that efficacy dosing in sick cats remains to be established (Ullom-Minnich 2022, PMID 35524963).

⭐ **A practical asymmetry for a fragile patient**: for a cat in whom oral pilling is itself risky (pharyngeal disease, airway compromise, restraint intolerance), the *route* can matter more than the drug — a monthly IM injection folded into an existing visit replaces thirty daily oral manipulations. That is a logistics observation, not an efficacy claim; no feline data compare the routes.

## 3. Monitoring draws blood, and blood is iron

The same safety study documented something with direct relevance to any intensively monitored cat: **serial venipuncture alone, at volumes below recommended limits, produced anemia and functional iron deficiency in healthy research cats** (Ullom-Minnich 2022).

> ⭐ **The circular trap**: a cat monitored closely for anemia is bled repeatedly *because* of the anemia, and the bleeding contributes to the thing being monitored. Consolidating every panel into one draw, and asking whether each scheduled recheck changes a decision, is a legitimate iron-conservation measure — and costs nothing.

---

## 4. Functional versus absolute deficiency — the distinction that decides whether iron helps

Absolute deficiency (depleted stores) and functional deficiency (stores present but sequestered by inflammation) call for different responses, and cats make the distinction unusually hard:

- Functional iron deficiency is **common** in cats with chronic gastrointestinal disease, where reticulocyte hemoglobin content correlated positively with serum iron (rs = .71) (Hunt & Jugan 2021, PMID 33226151).
- ⚠️ Species-specific obstacles, reported in the Betting 2022 full text: feline bone marrow does not stain for hemosiderin the way canine marrow does, and serum ferritin correlates only weakly with liver iron in cats — so the usual gold standards are degraded in this species. 【figures from the full text, not the abstract — verify before relying on them】
- The workable panel is serum iron + TIBC → transferrin saturation (± species-specific ferritin where available), added to a draw that is happening anyway.
- ⭐ **Reticulocyte indices do not separate the two forms** — a low value flags iron-limited erythropoiesis without saying why. If TSAT is normal, supplementation loses its rationale; that single add-on test can retire a daily medication.

---

## 5. The human reference point, for calibration only

In human chemotherapy-induced anemia (Cochrane review, 8 RCTs, n = 2087): iron added to ESAs improved hematopoietic response overall, **but the oral-iron subgroup showed no evidence of benefit** (RR 1.04, P = 0.68), **no trial has tested iron alone versus ESA alone**, and none of the included RCTs reported overall survival (Mhaskar 2016, PMID 26845108). ⚠️ Human data, ESA co-treatment context — imported here only to calibrate expectations: the combination closest to common feline practice (oral iron, no ESA, inflammation-driven anemia) is precisely the cell with the weakest human evidence.

**PMID 35090061** · Betting A 2022
> Iron deficiency was diagnosed in 9/64 cats (14%).
> A receiver-operating characteristic curve analysis yielded a moderate discriminatory value for CHr in this diagnosis: area under the curve [AUC] = .75 (95% confidence interval, 0.48-0.89); P = .006; sensitivity 67%, specificity 82% for a cutoff of 15.9 pg.
> CHr added moderate value to the diagnosis of iron deficiency in cats with CKD.

**PMID 33617045** · Keiner M 2020
> RIs for RETIC-HGB and CHr were 12.5-18.0 and 14.0-19.9 pg, respectively.
> RETIC-HGB and CHr were moderately correlated (rs = 0.59) with a bias of -1.2 picograms (pgs).
> Using the lower RI limits resulted in a low sensitivity and relatively high specificity to diagnose ILE in cats.
> The moderate correlation between RETIC-HGB and CHr is likely due to species differences and different methodology.

**PMID 22942439** · Naigamwalla DZ 2012
> Iron deficiency anemia in dogs and cats is usually caused by chronic blood loss and can be discovered incidentally as animals may have adapted to the anemia.
> Severe iron deficiency is characterized by a microcytic, hypochromic, potentially severe anemia with a variable regenerative response.
> ⚠️ The dose figures cited in the body text (ferrous sulfate; iron dextran 50 mg/cat IM q3–4wk; test dose; never IV) are from this review's body text, not its abstract, and were not verifiable against the archived record.

**PMID 35524963** · Ullom-Minnich R 2022
> No cat experienced an acute drug reaction.
> IV iron sucrose was well tolerated in healthy cats but was associated with transient increase in the systemic inflammatory marker SAA.
> Efficacy evaluation of dose based on iron deficit is needed in sick cats.
> Despite cumulative blood draw volume below recommended limits, anemia and FID were observed, which has important implications for experimental designs and serial hematologic monitoring.

**PMID 33226151** · Hunt A 2021
> Functional iron deficiency was common in cats with chronic GI disease.

**PMID 26845108** · Mhaskar R 2016
> We did not find any trial comparing iron alone versus ESAs alone in people with CIA.
> None of the included RCTs reported overall survival.

**PMID 21872790** · Chalhoub S 2011
> The use of ESAs has been associated with a number of complications, such as iron deficiency, hypertension, arthralgia, fever, seizures, polycythemia and pure red cell aplasia (PRCA).
> There is limited published evidence to date to underpin the use of ESAs in cats.
> ⚠️ The feline iron dose figures cited in the body text (oral ferrous sulfate 50–100 mg/cat/day; iron dextran 50 mg/cat IM q3–4wk; never IV) are from this review's body text, not its abstract, and were not verifiable against the archived record.
